# LINDKON ApS: Independent B2B Growth, Marketing & Sales Audit

**Date:** 3 October 2026
**Scope:** research and strategic analysis only. No changes have been made to lindkon.com or any code.
**Evidence basis:** public search-index evidence and public registries/press. The live website could not be opened from the research environment (see §0). **Read §0 before acting on the website findings.**

---

## Executive summary

**Diagnosis.**
- LINDKON is a profitable, owner-led SME. Gross profit was about DKK 6.7M in FY24/25, with 17–18 people.
- It has two genuinely distinctive assets:
  - its **own-design, Czech-made sign hardware**: a design-protected, weld-free galvanised stand range;
  - a **25+-year Czech production network with Czech engineers**.
- It also has a large installed base: the MD has cited ~2,500 customers, mainly estate agents and chains.
- **Its commercial communication uses none of these.** LINDKON presents itself as a broad, "cheapest" large-format printer. That is the most crowded and price-transparent arena in Danish print, and at least four online sign shops make the same "cheapest" claim. The website it does this through currently **undermines trust**:
  - a "not functional" notice on indexed product pages;
  - contradictory factory counts;
  - no cases or references;
  - legacy pages still indexed;
  - industrial outsourcing buried among beachflags.
- Gross-profit growth slowed to ~5% in FY24/25 after +40% the year before, despite a strong 2025 housing market. That is a warning sign worth explaining with internal data.

**The answer is not "more marketing". It is a sharper growth model with three engines:**

1. **Property-sector sign systems (core + growth).** Steel hardware + print for estate agents (defend, deepen, lock in), developers/project sellers (primary new-account growth), commercial property (rail/frame systems) and selected contractors.
   - **Won account by account** (ABM: research → personalised physical sample → phone → meeting/factory visit).
   - **Retained through programmes and a customer ordering portal**, then grown through account management.
   - The market is finite and identifiable: 1,500 estate-agent locations, with six to seven chain head offices influencing ~65% of them. That suits targeted selling far better than broad advertising.
2. **Self-service standard products.** Stands, agent plates/window signs, CVR site signs and simple banners, sold through a working webshop with prices, email to existing customers and *narrow* search ads. **No sales time.** Commodities that can't be sold profitably this way get "price on request" and no promotion.
3. **LINDKON Industri (outsourcing) as a separate brand.** Bilingual site, capability matrix, QA process, cases, one consistent factory count, MD-led outreach to SME manufacturers. A comparable Danish competitor (KKP Adviser & Produktion) shows how much better the same proposition can be presented.

**Fix immediately (weeks, low cost):**
- Remove the "not functional" notice (or finish checkout).
- Drop "Danmarks billigste priser".
- Use one factory number everywhere.
- Add a structured quote form, sample request and tracking (CRM, GA4, Search Console, call tracking).
- Publish 3–5 cases with photos and numbers.
- 301-redirect legacy URLs and retire the 2018 catalogue.
- Clean up outdated directory entries and the "advertising agency" industry classification.
- Re-verify the "Guld-AAA" claim. Public filings show equity falling from ~DKK 6.1M to ~0.9M in FY24/25.
- Get legal review of green-claim wording, which is now regulated more strictly under EU Directive 2024/825, applying from 27 Sep 2026.

**Channel verdict (for LINDKON specifically), in order of priority:**
1. Account-based outbound (phone + physical samples + meetings), account management and the ordering portal.
2. Targeted SEO segment pages. LINDKON already ranks #1–8 for "stålstativ til skilte" and #3 for "projektskilte", but is invisible for "mæglerskilte".
3. Two small, tightly tracked Google Ads tests.
4. People-led LinkedIn.
5. **Not now:** Meta ads, broad Google/Performance Max, broad LinkedIn Ads, trade-show exhibiting for signs, sponsorships, national retail-POS acquisition, generic blogging.

**Note on cold email:** Danish marketing law generally restricts unsolicited marketing email to businesses as well as consumers. Design outbound around phone and physical mail, and have this verified by a lawyer.

**ESG:** valuable as a **procurement qualifier and as product data**, not as a slogan. The useful forms are:
- a VSME-format report pack;
- documented CO2e per product family;
- PVC-free documentation, which matters for public buyers following POGI;
- a circular stand/frame reuse programme.

The "Going Green" badge and phrases like "PVC-free vinyl" and "more environmentally friendly" need rewording.

**90 days:** three weeks of foundations (data sprint, 10 customer interviews, hygiene, measurement, proof assets, kits, legal checks), followed by **four experiments**, each with an explicit decision rule:
1. Developer/project-signage ABM.
2. Estate-agent chain programme + reorder-portal pilot.
3. Outsourcing pilot.
4. A high-intent search test.

**What can't be decided without internal data:**
- How many of the 1,500 estate-agent locations already buy from LINDKON, and their spend per shop. This decides whether the core strategy is "acquire" or "deepen".
- Gross margin by segment.
- The cause of the FY24/25 slowdown.
- Outsourcing economics and owner-time dependence.
- Production capacity.

## 1. Business positioning

### 1.1 What LINDKON actually sells, seen from the buyer's side

LINDKON describes itself through **product categories**: banners, stands, signs, foil, flags, A-boards, project signage. Buyers don't buy categories. They buy an outcome for a recurring situation. Re-cut by buyer job, LINDKON runs **five different businesses** under one URL:

| Buyer situation | What the buyer is really buying | LINDKON's relevant assets | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Estate agent** has a new listing | On-site "for sale" presence in chain branding within days, with no admin: stand + plate + sticker/window sign, replenished continuously | Own-design weld-free galvanised stands (21 models), PP/sandwich plates, flap/angle window signs, stock + 1–2 day delivery | **Recurring consumable + durable hardware** |
| **Developer / project seller / contractor** starts a project | Project visibility and information from day 1. Weather-proof for 1–3 years. Installed without the buyer's involvement | Large sandwich/PP boards, mesh fence banners, banners on rails, partner installers, metal frames via the Czech network [I] | **Project-based, repeat across projects** |
| **Commercial agent / property owner** has vacant space | Faster leasing. A visible "TIL LEJE" message that can be swapped when status changes | Banners with rail system/banner frames (a legacy page targets *erhvervsmæglere*) | **Recurring per vacancy, hardware-anchored** |
| **Shop / SME / club** wants visibility | Cheap, quick, decent-looking promo items | Foil, A-boards, beachflags, kiosk flags, banners | **Commodity, one-off, price-driven** |
| **Industrial company** needs custom parts | A managed, lower-cost, lower-risk CEE supply chain for small/medium series, with an accountable Danish counterpart | 25+ years of Czech network, Czech engineers, metal/plastic/textile breadth | **High-value recurring B2B service, completely different buyer** |

### 1.2 Is the positioning too broad?

**Yes. The problem is not the number of products, though. It is that the products are presented without a buyer.** Breadth is normal for a regional producer. What hurts is that:

1. **The headline proposition is a product list** ("leading supplier of banners, stands, signs and large stickers"). The tagline is a value statement ("Kvalitetsbevidst og frem for alt ordentlige!"). Neither says *for whom* or *why LINDKON rather than the next supplier*.
2. **Two contradictory value claims sit side by side**: "quality-conscious and decent" versus "Denmark's cheapest prices on signs". A cheapest-price claim invites the most price-sensitive buyers and anchors negotiations downwards. It is also a superlative that must be documentable under Danish marketing law. **I recommend removing it.**
3. **Industrial outsourcing sits in the same navigation as beachflags.** A purchasing manager at a machine builder lands on what looks like a sign shop. An estate agent sees steel outsourcing. Each audience gets noise, and the outsourcing business loses credibility (see §3.4).

### 1.3 Strongest differentiators (evidence-based)

Ranked by how defensible they are:

1. **Hardware that LINDKON owns: design-protected, weld-free, galvanised steel stands (21 models), presumably made through LINDKON's Czech production network** [V for the stand claims; I for where they are made]. Few sign printers control their own metal product line. This gives cost control, product IP, and a *razor-and-blades* dynamic: the stand stays at the agent or site, and the plates and stickers get re-ordered.
2. **Metal + print from one supplier.** The Czech network can make custom frames, rails, stands, pylons and sign structures, and LINDKON prints and assembles in Aabybro [I – inferred from the two capabilities; confirm practical lead times and minimum quantities]. Pure printers cannot do this cheaply, and pure metal shops cannot print.
3. **A 25+-year Czech manufacturing network with Czech engineers.** This is genuinely rare for a Danish SME and well timed for the nearshoring trend. But it is a different business, and right now it is presented with almost no proof (§3, §12).
4. **A consistently profitable track record and a modern 1,100 m² production facility (2019).** This matters to procurement departments that screen supplier risk. Today the "Guld-AAA" credit claim is used as a generic badge rather than as an argument for multi-year chain or framework agreements, and it needs re-verification (§11.5).
5. **Documented ESG work**: ESG reports, Code of Conduct, the GRAKOM/HK CSR code, PP/PVC-free alternatives, solar. This is above average for a small sign producer [I – see §12 for competitor comparison]. Today it is a values page rather than a procurement-ready document pack (§11).
6. **Stock and speed on standard estate-agent items** (in stock, 1–2 working days).

### 1.4 What is commoditised

- **Banners, beachflags, kiosk flags, A-boards, roll-ups, window/floor stickers and standard PVC/PP plates in standard sizes.** These are sold by web-to-print players with instant prices, calculators and next-day delivery (see §12). LINDKON cannot win these on price from a quote-by-email model, and it should not try to win them with paid media.
- **Generic "large-format print"** as a capability claim. Every competitor claims it.

### 1.5 Where LINDKON could command higher value

Margin comes from **reducing the buyer's coordination cost and risk**, not from print per m²:

- **Sign *systems* for multi-location buyers.** A standardised stand/frame/rail + print + replenishment programme + ordering portal, for estate-agent chains, franchise networks, property administrators and developers. The value is consistency, speed and zero admin, so price per item becomes secondary.
- **Project-signage packages for developers and contractors.** Specify → produce → deliver → install (via partners) → swap → take down → take back/recycle. Sell per project or as a framework agreement.
- **Custom metal + print builds** that web printers cannot quote: special stands, frames, pylons, sports-facility structures.
- **Procurement-ready documentation** (ESG/VSME report, material declarations, CO2e estimates per product family, Code of Conduct, credit rating). It shortens supplier approval at large buyers, which is a real but secondary value driver.
- **Outsourcing**, priced on total cost of ownership versus Danish production, not on piece price alone.

## 2. Ideal customer profiles

### 2.1 Market facts that anchor the analysis

- **Estate agents [V]:**
  - **1,500 member locations** (Dansk Ejendomsmæglerforening, July 2026). EDC 247, Nybolig 202, home 162, danbolig 149, RealMæglerne 81, LokalBolig 66, Estate 65; independents/small chains 431.
  - **Ownership is concentrated**: Nykredit (Nybolig + Estate, 267), Realkredit Danmark/Danske Bank group (home), Nordea (danbolig), Jyske Bank (co-owns LokalBolig). EDC is agent-owned.
  - **Six to seven chain head offices influence ~65% of all locations** [I from V figures].
  - **~100,300 owner-occupied homes were sold in 2025 (+17%)**: ~60,000 houses, ~21,300 apartments, plus ~11,300 summer houses. Strong activity continued into 2026 [V].
  - **No public evidence was found of chain-wide sign agreements or chain ordering portals** [?]. The two-level pattern (HQ sets the design, shops order and pay) is inferred from the franchise/owner structures [I].
- **LINDKON's own base [V – press, 2019]:** about **2,500 customers**, mainly estate agents and estate-agent chains, larger retail chains and contractors. About a dozen outsourcing customers. **If a large share of the 1,500 estate-agent locations already buy from LINDKON, growth in that segment comes from *share of wallet and lock-in*, not new logos.** This is the single most important number to pull from internal data (§15).
- **Commercial property:** office vacancy ~7.7% nationally, retail vacancy at a record low (Q3 2026, figures to confirm) [V – unpinned]. That means steady rather than growing to-let demand.
- **Construction:** project-apartment sales rose in Q1 2026 [V]. Signage *requirements* exist in several places:
  - EU-funded projects must display durable billboards/plaques above certain cost thresholds (Regulation (EU) 2021/1060, Art. 50) [I – verify].
  - Large sites must display the prior notice (Directive 92/57/EEC) [I – verify].
  - **Since 1 January 2013, construction sites on private land must display the name and CVR number of every firm working there.** Exemptions include contracts ≤ DKK 50,000 incl. VAT and one-day jobs. Fine ≥ DKK 5,000 [V – Skat/TEKNIQ guidance via search summaries]. This creates steady, standardised demand at the low-price end.
- **Chains/franchise:** template-based ordering portals already exist in the Danish market. OK Plus uses a store portal built by a print/communication supplier, and LaserTryk offers "BrandCentral" template ordering [V]. **The portal model is proven, and LINDKON would be catching up, not inventing.**
- **LINDKON's scale [V]:**
  - Gross profit **DKK 4.5M (FY22/23) → 6.4M (FY23/24) → 6.7M (FY24/25)**.
  - Net result ~DKK 1.2–1.8M.
  - 17–18 employees.
  - Revenue is not published.

  **Growth investments must be sized accordingly.** One additional salesperson, assuming a fully loaded cost of ~DKK 0.65–0.75M, is roughly 10% of current gross profit.

#### A. Estate-agent chains and multi-shop agents (head office + shops)

| Sales complexity | Medium. Few HQ decision makers, but incumbents are sticky and shops are autonomous |

## 3. Website: lindkon.com as a B2B sales asset

*Evidence comes from the search index (§0). Design and visual quality could not be assessed. This section is deliberately about commercial function.*

### 3.1 Verdict

**lindkon.com works as a digital brochure for buyers who already know LINDKON. It does not work as a sales asset for buyers who don't.** It describes products. It does not segment buyers, prove capability, capture qualified demand, support large deals or enable re-ordering. Several self-inflicted credibility problems actively cost trust:

1. **A "not functional" notice on indexed product pages** ("SIDEN ER VED AT BLIVE BYGGET OP OG DERFOR IKKE FUNKTIONEL") on pages that show prices and quantity tiers. A procurement-minded visitor reads this as "this supplier hasn't finished its own website". It also wastes the only transactional pages Google indexes. **Fix within days: hide the notice or finish the flow (§10).**
2. **Inconsistent core facts.** The Czech network is "about 40", "about 50" and "more than 50" factories on different pages. For the outsourcing business, where credibility is everything, this is damaging.
3. **"Denmark's cheapest prices on signs"** next to "quality-conscious". This is a mixed message and a legal exposure (§1.2).
4. **Zero proof**: no named customers, cases, project photos with context, testimonials, volumes or reviews were found.
5. **Legacy debris**: old ASP.NET pages and a 2018 catalogue PDF are still indexed.

### 3.2 Element-by-element

| Element | Assessment | Commercial consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **First impression / positioning** | Values tagline + product list. No buyer named, no outcome, no reason to choose | Visitors must work out relevance themselves. Most B2B visitors won't |
| **Navigation** | Product-led (Skilte, Bannere, Stativer, Gadesælgere, Folie, Flag, Projektskiltning, Skilte til vinduer) + Outsourcing, Sport, Going Green, Om, Kontakt | No entry point for "I am an estate agent / developer / commercial landlord / chain / industrial buyer" |

| **Outsourcing separated?** | No. One Danish page inside a sign-maker site | See 3.4 |

### 3.3 Recommended information architecture (direction, not design)

Organise the main navigation **by buyer, then by product**:

- **Ejendomsmæglere** (stands, plates, window signs, stickers; chain programme; reorder/portal; prices for standard items)
- **Byggeri & projektsalg** (project signs, fence/mesh banners, site boards, installation, take-down/take-back; project package request)
- **Erhvervsudlejning** (to-let banners, rail/frame systems, swap service)
- **Butikker & kæder** (facade/window foil, A-boards, flags — honest, simple, priced)
- **Produkter** (a full catalogue for people who search by product)
- **Bæredygtighed & dokumentation** (procurement pack: ESG/VSME report, CoC, material data sheets, CO2e method)
- **Om LINDKON** (factory, people, history, credit rating, **cases**)
- **LINDKON SPORT** kept as a sub-page, not a main-navigation peer (§2)

**Outsourcing moves to its own sub-brand/site** (3.4).

Every segment page needs: a one-line outcome promise, three proof points (named case, photo, number), standard items with prices where possible, a **segment-specific form** (fields that qualify: number of shops/projects/sites, timing, quantities), a sample-kit offer, and one named contact person.

### 3.4 Should outsourcing be separated? Yes, clearly.

The outsourcing buyer (purchasing or supply-chain manager, technical director) evaluates **risk**: quality system, process, capacity, engineering competence, references, delivery reliability, communication language, IP protection, total landed cost. Today the page offers a list of materials and three slightly contradictory sentences.

Recommendation: a separate site or sub-brand, for example a dedicated domain or `lindkon.com/industri` with its own navigation. Bilingual Danish/English at minimum (German and Swedish can come later if those markets are targeted). It needs:

- A capability matrix (processes × materials × tolerances × series sizes).
- A step-by-step process (RFQ → DFM → sample/first-article → series → QC → logistics).
- QA documentation (which certifications the partner factories hold).
- 3–5 anonymised or named cases with savings and lead times.
- The Czech engineering team (names, photos).
- A "send drawings for a quote" form with secure upload.
- One consistent factory count.

The print/sign site then only links to it ("LINDKON also runs industrial production in the Czech Republic →"). The link should work in the other direction too: **the steel capability should be used *as proof* on the sign site** ("our stands and frames are made through our Czech production network").

### 3.5 Quick wins vs. structural changes

**Quick wins (days to a few weeks, low cost):**
1. Remove or resolve the "not functional" notice.
2. Pick one factory number and use it everywhere.
3. Remove "Danmarks billigste priser".
4. Add a structured quote form with segment fields.
5. Add a sample request.
6. Add three cases with photos.
7. 301-redirect the legacy URLs and retire the 2018 catalogue.
8. Write unique titles and meta descriptions.
9. Install measurement (§15).

**Structural (part of the planned redesign):**
- Buyer-led architecture.
- A separate outsourcing site.
- Segment landing pages.
- A procurement documentation hub.
- A customer portal decision (§10).

**Note on the redesign brief:** the repository README sets a scope guard ("no webshop, customer portal…"). This commercial analysis suggests the **customer-ordering capability may be the most important part of the growth model** for chain and repeat customers (§10). That scope decision should be revisited *before* the redesign locks the architecture. That doesn't mean building a portal now. It means not designing a site that would make one awkward to add later.

### 4.2 What this means

## 5. Google Ads / paid search

### 5.1 Does it make economic sense?

**Only in a few narrow, high-intent clusters, with dedicated landing pages and real conversion tracking, and only after the site's conversion basics are fixed.** Without keyword-planner volumes and LINDKON's margins (neither available to this audit), the economics can only be framed, not proven:

> **Allowable cost per acquired customer ≈ expected first-year gross profit × payback tolerance.**
> **Allowable cost per lead ≈ allowable cost per customer × lead-to-win rate.**

- A **commodity order** (A-board, beachflag, single banner) has a small gross profit. At realistic Danish B2B click costs and conversion rates it rarely pays back, especially against webshops with instant prices and "cheapest" claims [I].
- A **developer, chain or commercial-property account** can be worth tens of thousands of DKK in gross profit over its life. Paying a few hundred DKK per qualified lead can be very profitable *if* leads are qualified and followed up [I].
- **Outsourcing** search volume is probably tiny, but a single series customer can justify a large cost per lead. The issue is volume, not cost [I].

### 5.2 Categories

|---|---|
| "mæglerskilte", "skilte til ejendomsmæglere", "stativ til mæglerskilt", "stålstativ skilt" (and a **Shopping campaign for stands once the shop works**) | Generic "skilte", "bannere", "print", "storformat print", "reklame" |
| "projektskilte", "byggeskilt bygherre", "bygherreskilt", "salgsskilt nybyggeri", "byggeskilt med stålramme" | "beachflag", "A-skilt", "roll-up", "vinduesfolie", "gulvstickers" (consumer/SMB price shoppers vs. web-to-print) |

### 5.3 Landing-page requirements

One page per cluster, never the homepage. Each page needs:

- A headline that names the buyer.
- Product/package options with "fra"-prices or price logic.
- Lead times.
- Photos of real installations.
- 2–3 cases.
- An ESG/document link.
- A short qualifying form: role, company, number of shops/projects, quantities, deadline, file upload.
- Phone with call tracking.
- A sample-kit offer.

**The "not functional" notice must be gone before a single krone is spent.**

### 5.4 Campaign segmentation

- **Separate campaigns per segment/cluster** (Mæglere / Projekt & byg / Erhvervsudlejning / Outsourcing / Brand), each with its own budget, landing page and conversion goal.
- **Exact and phrase match first.** Broad match only later, with conversion-value bidding and solid negatives.
- **Geography:** Denmark. Consider a Jutland-first test for installation-heavy offers.
- **Schedule:** weekdays and business hours for B2B lead campaigns.
- **Assets:** call, sitelink (cases, ESG pack, sample kit) and lead-form assets where appropriate.
- **Shopping (Merchant Center)** for steel stands and standard estate-agent items once the webshop works. LINDKON already ranks organically for "stålstativ til skilte", so test incrementality before scaling.
- **Avoid Performance Max until** there is enough conversion data and offline conversion import. Without both, PMax tends to drift into low-quality placements and brand traffic [I].

- **"til salg"** attracts home buyers: exclude hus, huse, lejlighed, sommerhus, grund, bolig, boliger til salg, boligsiden, boliga.

- **"banner"** attracts digital banners: exclude web, online, gif, html5, annonce, bannerannonce, youtube, twitch, canva, størrelse pixels.

- **"outsourcing"** attracts IT/HR/BPO: exclude IT, løn, bogføring, kundeservice, callcenter, HR, rekruttering.

### 7.5 The contact sequence, and a Danish legal constraint

**Important:** the Danish Marketing Practices Act's ban on unsolicited *electronic* marketing (email, SMS, automated calls) is generally understood to apply **to businesses as well as consumers**. That makes Denmark stricter than many countries, where cold B2B email is common. Phone calls to businesses and addressed letters are permitted. **Have this checked by a lawyer, but plan the outbound motion around phone + physical mail + meetings, with email only after consent or an existing relationship** [I – legal verification recommended]. Treat LinkedIn messaging conservatively too: use it to connect and share relevant content, not for mass pitching.

Illustrative sequence for a Tier A/B account (3–6 weeks):

1. **Day 0:** research. Pick 2–3 contacts and one specific trigger or reason.
2. **Day 1:** **physical kit or letter** with a personalised mock-up (their logo on a LINDKON stand/board, or their project render on a mini project board; a rail-system sample for commercial property; a capability box with real parts for outsourcing).
3. **Day 3–5:** **phone call** referencing the kit. Goal: a 20-minute meeting or a concrete brief/RFQ. Ask permission to email material.
4. **Day 5–10:** consented email with 1–2 relevant cases + ESG/procurement pack link + meeting link.
5. **Day 10–20:** second call / LinkedIn connection with relevant content. Offer a factory visit (Aabybro) or video tour.
6. **Day 20–40:** proposal, sample order or pilot (e.g. one project, one region of shops, one outsourced part).
7. **No response:** nurture quarterly with a new case. Re-trigger on new projects or listings.

### 7.6 Meetings, follow-up and CRM pipeline

- **Meeting formats:** 20-minute video discovery → site/factory visit for Tier A → proposal review.
- **Follow-up rule:** every inbound or outbound conversation gets a next step with a date in the CRM. A sample kit without a call within 5 working days counts as wasted spend.
- **Pipeline stages** (per segment):

  Target account → Contacted → Conversation → Qualified (fit + need + timing + budget owner) → Brief/RFQ received → Quote/proposal sent → Won / Lost (reason) → Onboarded (templates/portal/price agreement) → Active (reordering) → Expanded (new shops/projects/products)

- **Required CRM fields:** segment, tier, source/channel, trigger, decision-maker roles, estimated annual value, quote value, gross margin (from the quote), loss reason, competitor.

### 8.2 Field vs. inside sales

- **Inside-sales-first, with planned field days.** From Aabybro, most of the Danish market (Aarhus, Triangle Region, Funen, Zealand) is far away. Phone, video, email, LinkedIn and sample kits cover first contact efficiently. **Field meetings should be reserved for Tier-A accounts** (§7) and bundled into planned regional days, for example two Copenhagen/Zealand days a month once pipeline justifies it.
- **Factory visits in Aabybro are a sales weapon.** For chain buyers, developers and especially outsourcing prospects, seeing production and stock is persuasive. Offer it explicitly.

### 8.3 Hunting vs. account management

- **Separate the roles conceptually even if one person starts with both.** Hunters measured on new accounts and first-year margin. Account management measured on retention, reorder frequency, share of wallet, and portal adoption.
- Likely first configuration, given what's visible of the team [I]:
  - **Owner/MD**: Tier-A relationships + outsourcing.
  - **One new B2B hunter** (inside + field) for the sign-system segments.
  - **Existing sales support** evolves into account management + portal/customer success.
  - **Outsourcing technical sales** handled by the MD with the Czech engineers until a pilot proves demand.

  Do not hire several salespeople before the 90-day tests show which segments convert (§16).
- **Compensation on gross margin, not revenue.** Revenue-based commission encourages discounting, which is exactly the habit LINDKON should break.

### 8.4 What salespeople should NOT spend time on

- Manually quoting standard items. Publish prices or use a calculator/portal.
- Chasing artwork and proofs. Use templates/portal and a graphic-support workflow.
- Handling small one-off orders.
- Cold-calling random SMEs or shops.
- Running social media "for visibility".
- Trade fairs without pre-booked meetings.
- Sports clubs without a sponsor-funded model.
- Building their own prospect lists from scratch. That is a marketing/research task (§7).

## 10. B2B e-commerce / customer portal

### 10.1 Current state

LINDKON has a WooCommerce catalogue (`/vare/`, `/vare-kategori/staalstativer/`). It shows prices, quantity tiers and stock/delivery for steel stands. The indexed product pages also say the site is "being built and therefore not functional" [V – verify live]. No customer login, chain-specific catalogue, artwork templates or reorder function was found. In practice it is a **half-public price list with a broken promise**.

### 10.2 Is "salesperson wins the account → tailored ordering → self-service reorders" the strongest model?

**For the core estate-agent and multi-location business, very probably yes.** It is also the model that best fits LINDKON's real advantages. The reasoning:

- **The purchase is repetitive and template-based.** Every listing needs the same stand/plate/sticker in chain branding with a few variable fields. Every project needs the same family of boards. Repetitive, templated orders are where portals beat email and phone on cost-to-serve and on speed.
- **The decision to *choose* a supplier is made once and centrally (chain HQ, developer's marketing, property administrator). Thousands of *orders* are placed locally afterwards** (shop assistants, project managers). A salesperson is needed for the first. A portal is better for the second.
- **Switching costs rise when the customer's templates, approved designs, price agreement, order history and delivery addresses live in LINDKON's system**, and when their stands and rails are LINDKON hardware. That is the defensible moat a sub-scale producer can build. It cannot build one on price.
- **It frees scarce people.** Sales support stops re-typing orders and chasing artwork.

**Where it is *not* the right model:**
- Commodity one-off buyers (an A-board for a café). A public price list or simple shop is enough, or nothing at all if LINDKON isn't price-competitive there.
- Outsourcing. That is RFQ- and engineering-driven and needs a secure drawing-upload and quote workflow, not a shop.
- Small clubs.

### 10.3 What the portal must do (minimum lovable version)

| Capability | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Customer-specific login, catalogue and **contract prices** | Chains must see only *their* approved products at *their* prices |
| **Templates with locked branding + editable fields** (address, price, agent name, "SOLGT" overlays) and automatic print-ready PDF | Removes artwork back-and-forth, the biggest hidden cost in sign production |
| Multi-user accounts: HQ admin, shop/project users, optional approval step | Matches how chains and developers actually buy |
| Delivery to shop or site; **central invoicing or per-shop invoicing** (EAN/OIOUBL for public buyers) | Chains and public customers require this |
| Order history and one-click reorder | Drives repeat frequency |
| Stock items (stands, accessories) and made-to-order items in one basket | Captures hardware and consumables together |
| Per-order documentation (material, PVC-free, take-back option; later CO2e estimate) | Turns ESG into an operational advantage for procurement |
| Reporting to HQ (spend per shop, per project) | Gives the chain buyer a reason to mandate the portal |

### 10.4 Build or buy, and sequencing

- **Don't build a big platform first.** Run a **pilot with 1–2 existing customers who already re-order often** (an estate-agent chain or franchise group, or a developer). Use a password-protected customer area on WooCommerce with B2B pricing and simple templates, or a hosted web-to-print/B2B portal product. Many exist. Choose by template editor, approval flows, ERP/accounting integration and price, not by feature lists.
- **Measure:** orders per customer per month, order handling minutes, artwork-correction rate, reorder share, customer satisfaction, and whether HQ mandates the portal to its shops.
- **Scale only if the pilot shows a lower cost-to-serve and a higher reorder frequency.**
- **Public shop:** keep it only for products where LINDKON is genuinely competitive and in stock (stands, standard estate-agent plates and window signs). For commodity print that LINDKON doesn't want to fight for, show "price on request" and route to the quote form.

### 10.5 Risks

- **Portal without account wins = an empty shop.** The portal is a *retention and expansion* tool. It doesn't generate demand by itself.
- **Integration effort** with accounting/ERP (unknown system [?]).
- **Template governance.** Each chain's design manual must be implemented correctly. That is a one-off cost per account, recovered over the contract.

## 11. ESG / Going Green: is it commercially valuable?

### 11.1 What LINDKON has (public evidence)

- **Product side:** PP signs presented as a PVC alternative, PVC-free banner material, and galvanised, weld-free reusable steel stands [V].
- **Operations side:** expanded solar installation, EV charging on own solar, district heating stated as 99% renewable [V].
- **Documentation side:**
  - ESG Rapport 2023 and 2024 (the first report and climate account presented in 2024, prepared with Erhvervshus Nordjylland / Vækst Jammerbugt) [V].
  - Code of Conduct referenced [V].
  - Signatory of the graphic-industry CSR code (GRAKOM/HK, 2022) [V].
- **People side:** a LinkedIn profile lists an **"ESG Controller og Data Analytiker"** at LINDKON [V]. That is unusual capacity for a company of 17–18 people.
- **Missing publicly:** product-level CO2 data, method notes, certifications (ISO 14001, Svanemærket, FSC/PEFC), supplier code for the Czech partners, and a 2025 report in VSME format [? – not found in index].

### 11.2 The regulatory reality in late 2026 (why "ESG as a sales weapon" needs care)

- **CSRD has been cut back sharply.** The EU Omnibus I directive (Directive (EU) 2026/470, in force 18 Mar 2026) limits CSRD to companies with **>1,000 employees *and* >€450m turnover**, from FY2027 [V]. Denmark has already exempted many first-wave companies and has a further simplification bill in progress (consultation July–August 2026) [V]. **Far fewer of LINDKON's customers will be *legally* forced to collect supplier ESG data.**
- **The "value-chain cap" protects SMEs.** Companies with ≤1,000 employees may decline sustainability-reporting data requests that go beyond the **VSME** voluntary standard, now an EU delegated regulation (2026/1560, in force 24 Sep 2026, cap applying from FY2027) [V]. **VSME is becoming the common currency** for SME supplier data in Denmark. Erhvervsstyrelsen recommends it and provides free templates/tools, and Finanstilsynet points banks to it [V].
- **Demand doesn't disappear, though.**
  - Banks are integrating ESG risk into credit assessment [V, secondary sources].
  - An SMVdanmark analysis (via secondary sources) found **~40% of SMEs receive customer requests for ESG data** [V, secondary].
  - Public buyers set minimum requirements. The Danish public green-procurement partnership (POGI) requires printed matter to meet **Svanemærket/EU Ecolabel criteria** and states **"all roll-ups must be purchased in PVC-free qualities"** [V].
  - **Svanemærket's printing criteria (041) explicitly cover banners and roll-ups, and exclude PVC** [V].
- **Green claims are now tightly regulated.** EU Directive 2024/825 ("empowering consumers for the green transition") **applies from 27 September 2026**. It bans:
  - generic environmental claims ("miljøvenlig", "grøn", "klimavenlig") without recognised excellent performance;
  - offset-based "climate neutral" product claims;
  - **sustainability labels not based on a certification scheme or public authority**;
  - presenting legal requirements as a distinctive feature.

  It is consumer law, but **Danish marketing law also governs business-to-business claims, which must be documentable**. LINDKON's website is also visible to consumers. Danish transposition details were not verified [I/?].

### 11.3 Commercial assessment

| Buyer | Is ESG a decision driver? | What it takes |
|---|---|---|
| Large companies / chains (private) | **Mostly a qualifier ("hygiene")**. It rarely decides a signage order alone, but it can disqualify a supplier or tip a close decision [I] | A ready VSME report + Code of Conduct + product data on request, delivered in hours, not weeks |
| **Public buyers** (municipalities, regions, state) | **Minimum requirements**: PVC-free, Svanemærket/EU Ecolabel criteria, FSC/PEFC [V] | Eligibility. LINDKON's PVC-free range is an *entry ticket* to public signage work if it can document it |
| Developers / contractors | Weak but rising. Waste plans and DGNB processes favour reuse/take-back (not verified for signage specifically) [I] | Reusable stands/frames, take-back of plates, documented recycling route |
| Industrial outsourcing customers | Supplier code of conduct and traceability for CEE partners increasingly expected [I] | A supplier CoC signed by the Czech factories, audit routine, origin/traceability |

**Conclusion: ESG is commercially valuable for LINDKON in three specific forms (plus one optional), and as a generic "we are green" message it is close to worthless and now legally risky.**

1. **Procurement-ready pack (qualifier, low cost):**
   - An annual VSME-format report (basic module) as PDF + Excel.
   - A Klimakompasset-based climate account with a method note.
   - Code of Conduct + supplier code for Czech partners.
   - Material/product data sheets (REACH/SVHC declarations, PVC-free status, end-of-life guidance).
   - Solar and district-heating evidence.

   Put it behind a "Dokumentation til indkøb" page. Mention it in every chain/developer/public proposal.
2. **Product-level data (potential differentiator):** documented **kg CO2e estimates per product family** (per m² banner, per sign plate, per steel stand) that customers can drop into their own Scope-3 work. Few small sign producers can supply this [I]. LINDKON appears to have the in-house capability (ESG controller). Publish the method and the uncertainty honestly.
3. **Circular hardware programme (differentiator with operational substance):**
   - Steel stands and frames that are **reused, refurbished, re-plated or bought back**.
   - Take-back of project-signage plates *if* a real, documented recycling route exists.
   - Reuse rates tracked and reported per customer.

   This fits construction projects and estate-agent chains, which use stands until they break. It turns "Going Green" from a claim into a service.
4. **Optional, for public-sector ambitions:** evaluate a **Svanemærket 041 licence** for banners/roll-ups (PVC-free). Only pursue it if public buyers are a deliberate target segment (§2).

### 11.4 Claims to fix or avoid now

- **"Going Green" shown as a badge/label** on products. Under the 2024/825 rules on uncertified sustainability labels this is a risk. Use it as the *name of a product line* with specific, factual descriptions (e.g. "PP-plade, PVC-fri, 100% PP – kan genanvendes via [named route]"), not as a quality seal [I – legal review recommended].
- **"More environmentally friendly than PVC/sandwich"** without a documented basis (a comparative life-cycle claim). Replace with specific facts (material, PVC-free, recyclability route, weight).
- **"PVC-free vinyl"**: vinyl usually means PVC, so the wording is contradictory to technical buyers. Name the actual material.
- Any "CO2-neutral"/"klimaneutral" phrasing, unqualified "100% grøn strøm", or future targets without a plan.
- Presenting legal compliance (REACH etc.) as an advantage.

### 11.5 Related credibility point: "Guld-AAA"

Public filings indicate LINDKON ApS's **equity fell from about DKK 6.1M (FY2023/24) to about DKK 0.9M (FY2024/25)**, consistent with a distribution to the holding company [V figures / I interpretation]. Distributions are a normal owner decision. But **credit ratings and tender "financial capacity" screens assess the contracting entity**. Before "Guld-AAA" is used again in marketing or proposals, check which agency issues it and that it is current. If larger frameworks are pursued, decide whether a parent-company guarantee or group accounts should support bids.

## 14. The sales and marketing system

LINDKON should run **three acquisition engines on one shared backbone**. Running everything through one generic funnel is exactly how the current website ended up mixing estate agents, clubs and industrial buyers.

### 14.1 Engine 1: Property vertical (estate agents, developers, commercial property, selective contractors)

```
ICP & account list (DE chain data, developers, property managers, regional contractors)
   │  triggers: new project listings, permits, vacancies, rebrands, new shops, people changes
   ▼
ABM touch: personalised kit/letter ─► phone ─► consented email/LinkedIn ─► meeting / factory visit
   │                                   ▲
   │        inbound: segment pages (SEO + small Ads) ─► qualifying form / call
   ▼
Qualification (fit, need, timing, decision maker, annual value)
   ▼
Brief → quote/proposal (programme pricing, package, ESG pack, references) → sample/pilot
   ▼
Won → onboarding: templates, price agreement, delivery addresses, portal login
   ▼
Self-service reordering (portal/webshop) + account management (quarterly review, share of wallet)
   ▼
Expansion: more shops / projects / products (window signs, banners, rails, displays) + case + referral
```

### 14.2 Engine 2: Self-service standard products (stands, agent plates/window signs, CVR/site signs, simple banners)

```
Search / Shopping / existing-customer email ─► product pages with prices, stock, lead time
   ▼
Webshop checkout (invoice/card) ─► automated production ticket ─► delivery 1–2 days for stock items
   ▼
Reorder emails, bundles (stand + plate), upsell to programme if multi-site
```

**Zero sales time.** If an item can't be sold profitably this way, it gets "price on request" and no promotion.

### 14.3 Engine 3: Industrial outsourcing (separate brand/site)

```
Target list (SME manufacturers, DKK 10–200M revenue, metal-intensive; Jutland first)
   ▼
Capability letter/box + phone (MD) ─► LinkedIn (MD, Czech engineers) ─► trade-press cases
   ▼
RFQ with drawings (secure upload) ─► DFM feedback + Czech quote ─► first-article sample (paid pilot)
   ▼
Supplier approval/audit ─► framework agreement + call-offs ─► quarterly business review
   ▼
Expansion to more part numbers; reference case
```

### 14.4 Shared backbone

- **CRM** (single source of truth for accounts, contacts, opportunities, quotes and outcomes).
- **Case library** (photos, numbers, quotes, permissions).
- **Procurement pack** (VSME report, CoC, data sheets, CO2e method, credit/financial info).
- **Sample-kit inventory.**
- **Production/ERP data** (gross margin per order and customer).
- **Monthly revenue and pipeline review** by segment and engine.

### 14.5 Service levels that make the system credible

| Step | Standard |
|---|---|
| Inbound quote request answered | ≤ 4 working hours (same day) |
| Standard quote | ≤ 1 working day. Project proposal ≤ 3 working days |
| Sample kit follow-up call | ≤ 5 working days after delivery |
| Stock items shipped | 1–2 working days (already promised) |
| Outsourcing RFQ first response | ≤ 2 working days with DFM questions. Quote timing agreed per RFQ |

## 15. Measurement

### 15.1 Minimum commercial measurement system

| Metric | Definition (keep it simple and consistent) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Lead source** | First and latest touch: ABM-outbound, referral, SEO, Ads (campaign), LinkedIn, email, trade show, partner, existing customer | CRM (mandatory field) + UTM + "how did you hear about us" |
| **Qualified lead** | Fits a target segment + has a real need + timing ≤ 6 months + decision maker identified | CRM stage |
| **Opportunity** | A brief/RFQ with scope and estimated value | CRM |
| **Quote** | Value, gross margin %, date, segment | Quote tool/ERP → CRM |
| **Win/loss** | Outcome, reason (price, lead time, quality, relationship, spec, no decision), competitor | CRM (mandatory on close) |
| **Acquisition cost** | Direct spend (ads, kits, events, tools) + estimated sales time, per segment/channel | Finance + CRM |
| **Revenue** | Per customer, segment, product family, month | ERP/accounting |
| **Gross margin** | Per order and per customer (materials + external costs; ideally also production time) | ERP. **Essential: without it, every channel decision is guesswork** |
| **Account lifetime value** | Gross profit per customer over 12/24/36 months, cohort-based | ERP + CRM |
| **Repeat purchasing** | Orders per customer per period, reorder interval, % revenue from repeat customers, portal share | ERP/webshop |
| **Pipeline by segment** | Count and value of qualified opportunities by stage, segment and engine; win rate; cycle time | CRM |
| **Share of wallet (key accounts)** | LINDKON spend ÷ estimated total sign spend (from customer interviews/HQ data) | Account plans |
| **Website conversion** | Sessions by segment page → form submits, calls, sample requests, shop purchases; conversion rate per page | GA4 + call tracking |
| **Google Ads conversion** | Cost per qualified lead, cost per won customer, won gross profit ÷ ad cost (ROAS on margin), by campaign | Ads + offline conversion import |

**Tooling:**
- A light CRM (pipeline + mandatory fields).
- GA4 + Google Search Console + call tracking + Consent Mode v2.
- Offline conversion import to Google Ads.
- A monthly one-page dashboard by segment.

Don't buy a marketing-automation suite before the basics are used consistently.

### 15.2 What cannot be decided intelligently without internal data

1. **Revenue and gross margin by segment and product family.** Is estate-agent work high- or low-margin compared with project signage, banners or outsourcing? Where does the money actually come from?
2. **Customer concentration and penetration.**
   - How many of the ~1,500 estate-agent locations buy from LINDKON?
   - What does an average shop spend per year?
   - Which chains dominate LINDKON's revenue?

   *This decides whether the estate-agent strategy is "acquire" or "deepen and defend".*
3. **Why gross-profit growth slowed** (+40% in FY23/24, then about +5% in FY24/25 [V]) while Danish home sales rose ~17% in calendar 2025 [V]. Was it price pressure, share loss, capacity, a one-off in FY23/24, or a mix shift?
4. **Outsourcing economics:**
   - revenue, margin per account and number of active customers (~12 in 2019 [V]);
   - capacity of the Czech engineers;
   - how much owner time each account consumes.
5. **Current lead sources, win rates and cycle times.** Without them, no channel can be judged on cost per acquisition.
6. **Production capacity and utilisation** (machines, widths, shifts). Can LINDKON absorb a chain programme or large project volume without new investment? What are the maximum print width and the installation constraints?
7. **The cost-to-serve of small orders** (artwork handling, quoting, shipping). This decides what goes to the webshop and what gets "price on request".
8. **LINDKON SPORT's actual profitability.**
9. **The status of the "Guld-AAA" rating** after the FY2024/25 equity change, and the group structure relevant for tenders [V figures].
10. **The legal position** on B2B email marketing and on current green claims (lawyer review).

## 17. Final assessment

### A. Biggest missed opportunities

1. **Turning the estate-agent installed base into locked-in programmes.** LINDKON reportedly served ~2,500 customers, mostly agents and chains [V – 2019]. Yet there is no chain programme, no templates and no ordering portal, while competitors in adjacent markets already run store portals for chains (Stibo/Rosendahls, Effection, LaserTryk BrandCentral) [V]. This is retention and share of wallet left on the table.
2. **Developer and project signage as a complete package**: boards + steel frames + installation + updates + take-down/reuse. Search results are thin, LINDKON already ranks #3 for "projektskilte" [V], order values are higher, and estate agents (LINDKON's base) sell the projects.
3. **Metal + print as one offer.** LINDKON's own-design, Czech-made hardware (stands, frames, rails, potentially pylons) is the strongest differentiator against web printers and POS houses. Today it isn't connected to the sign proposition at all.
4. **Credible industrial outsourcing.** There is a 25+-year network, Czech engineers and a nearshoring tailwind, yet it is presented with one Danish page and contradictory numbers. A direct competitor (KKP) shows how much better the same idea can be sold [V].
5. **ESG as procurement-ready data and a circular hardware service**: VSME pack, product CO2e, stand reuse/take-back, PVC-free documentation for public buyers. This beats a values page.
6. **Commercial-property rail/frame systems**: a product LINDKON has, in a niche where one competitor dominates search [V], with a dedicated page that was abandoned in the migration.

### B. Biggest current weaknesses

1. **The website damages trust.**
   - A "not functional" notice on indexed product pages.
   - Contradictory factory counts.
   - No cases or references.
   - Legacy pages and a 2018 catalogue still indexed.
   - Danish only for an international service.
2. **Price-led messaging in a crowded price war.** "Danmarks billigste priser" is the same claim several online sign shops make [V]. It undermines the quality and service story and invites the most price-sensitive buyers.
3. **No segmentation**: estate agents, construction, clubs, shops and industrial buyers are mixed. Outsourcing is buried.
4. **No visible commercial system**: no dedicated sales/key-account role visible, no evidence of CRM or measurement, likely heavy reliance on the owner [I].
5. **Growth is flattening** (gross profit +5% in FY24/25 after +40%) [V]. There is exposure to the housing cycle and possible saturation in the core segment [I].
6. **Thin and outdated external footprint.** Directories show the old Pandrup address and 7 employees, and classify LINDKON as an "advertising agency" [V]. That doesn't match what industrial buyers or property buyers look for.
7. **Claims at risk**: the "Going Green" badge-style use, "PVC-free vinyl", unqualified comparisons, and the "Guld-AAA" currency after the FY24/25 equity change [V/I].

### C. Existing strengths to exploit harder

1. Own design-protected, weld-free galvanised **stand range (21 models)** with stock, 1–2 day delivery and a Czech cost base.
2. **Organic dominance for "stålstativ til skilte"** (#1–8) and a top-3 position for "projektskilte" [V] *despite* a weak site. This shows what precise product pages can do.
3. **Long estate-agent heritage and a large customer base**, a natural bridge into developers and commercial property.
4. **The Czech manufacturing network + engineers**: hardware innovation for signs *and* a separate industrial business.
5. **Modern own production (1,100 m², 2019)** and a profitable, owner-led company that can decide quickly.
6. **Real ESG capability** (reports, CoC, CSR code, ESG controller) that most small sign competitors don't appear to have [I].

### D. What should probably NOT receive significant marketing budget

- Broad Google campaigns on generic terms (skilte, bannere, print, storformat). Performance Max and Display at this stage.
- Promotion of commodity items (beachflags, A-boards, roll-ups, window foil, generic banners) against web-to-print players.
- Meta advertising (beyond tiny retargeting or local SPORT activity).
- LinkedIn Ads with broad targeting.
- Exhibiting at general print/sign trade shows.
- Sponsorships, and LINDKON SPORT promotion beyond a productised, low-touch offer.
- National retail-chain POS/campaign acquisition (logistics-heavy incumbents).
- A generic blog/content programme.
- "Cheapest price" campaigns.
- A big-bang redesign that leaves out segment pages, tracking and a portal-ready architecture.

### E. What requires internal data before a conclusion is possible

See §15.2. The decisive items:
1. Gross margin by segment/product/customer.
2. **Penetration of the 1,500 estate-agent locations and spend per shop.**
3. The cause of the FY24/25 slowdown.
4. Outsourcing economics and owner-time dependence.
5. Production capacity.
6. Cost-to-serve of small orders.
7. LINDKON SPORT profitability.
8. Rating status and the group structure for tenders.

### F. Proposed LINDKON B2B growth model

**Positioning (one sentence):**
> *LINDKON makes sign systems for the Danish property sector: steel hardware and print from its own factory and Czech production network, delivered nationwide and re-ordered in minutes. Separately, LINDKON Industri is the Danish-contracted Czech production partner for small and medium series.*

**Structure, in three engines (§14):**

1. **Property-sector sign systems (core + growth).**
   - Who: estate agents (defend, deepen, lock in), developers/project sellers (primary new-account growth), commercial property (bundle), selected contractors.
   - Sold account-based (ABM + one hunter + MD for Tier A).
   - Retained through chain/project programmes and a customer ordering portal.
   - Grown by account management (share of wallet: window signs, displays, banners, rails, project packages).
2. **Self-service standard products.**
   - Stands, agent plates and window signs, CVR/site signs, simple banners.
   - A working webshop with prices, email to existing customers, and targeted Shopping/Search where margins allow.
   - **No sales time.**
3. **LINDKON Industri (outsourcing).**
   - A separate brand and site (DA/EN) and a proof pack.
   - MD-led ABM to SME manufacturers.
   - RFQ → first article → framework.
   - Scale only if the 90-day pilot shows demand.

**Shared backbone:** CRM and measurement on gross margin, a case library, a procurement/ESG pack, and the Czech supply chain as the cost and capability engine behind all three.

**What "substantially more profitable revenue" could look like.** *ILLUSTRATIVE ONLY: every number below is an assumption to be replaced by internal data.* The point is to show how few wins are needed relative to LINDKON's scale (gross profit ≈ DKK 6.7M in FY24/25 [V]):

| Source | Illustrative assumption | Annual gross profit |
|---|---|---|
| Developer + commercial-property accounts | 20 active accounts × 3 projects/yr × DKK 30k revenue × 40% margin | ≈ DKK 0.7M |
| Estate-agent share of wallet / chain programme | 300 shops × DKK 3k extra revenue/yr × 45% margin | ≈ DKK 0.4M |
| Outsourcing | 3 new series customers × DKK 1.5M revenue × 15% margin | ≈ DKK 0.7M |
| **Total** | | **≈ DKK 1.8M (≈ +25–30%)** |
| Cost side | One hunter (fully loaded, assumed ~DKK 0.65–0.75M/yr) + kits, tools and a small Ads budget | |

If the 90-day experiments confirm even half of these unit assumptions, the model pays for a dedicated salesperson within the first year. If they don't, the experiments will have cost little, and they will show *which* engine to fund instead.
